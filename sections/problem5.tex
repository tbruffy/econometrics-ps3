The unrestricted MLEs are

\subsection*{(a) Likelihood ratio statistic}
The unrestricted estimates violate $H_0:\beta_m\leq\beta_w$,
so the constrained maximum is on the boundary
$\beta_m=\beta_w$. Its common mean is the pooled mean,
\[
\widetilde\beta_m=\widetilde\beta_w
=\frac{100(64)+100(63)}{200}=63.5.
\]
Relative to the unrestricted maximum, the residual sum of squares
increases by
\[
100(64-63.5)^2+100(63-63.5)^2=50.
\]
Writing $\Lambda=\sup_{H_0}L/\sup L$, we obtain
\[
\boxed{\Lambda=e^{-50/(2\cdot100)}=e^{-1/4},
\qquad LR=-2\log\Lambda=\frac{50}{100}=0.5.}
\]

\subsection*{(b) Decision at the $5\%$ level}
The standardized mean difference is
\[
Z=\frac{\bar Y_m-\bar Y_w}
{\sigma\sqrt{1/n_m+1/n_w}}
=\frac1{\sqrt2}\approx0.707107.
\]
In general, the inequality-constrained statistic is
$LR=\{\max(Z,0)\}^2$.
At the least favorable null boundary $\beta_m=\beta_w$,
$Z\sim N(0,1)$ exactly, giving
\[
LR\sim\frac12\chi_0^2+\frac12\chi_1^2,
\]
where $\chi_0^2$ is a point mass at zero.
The exact $5\%$ critical value is
\[
z_{0.95}^2=\chi^2_{1,0.90}\approx2.70554.
\]
Since $0.5<2.70554$, we \textbf{do not reject}
$H_0:\beta_m\leq\beta_w$.
This boundary calibration controls size throughout the composite
null, since negative values of $\beta_m-\beta_w$ reduce the
upper-tail rejection probability.

\subsection*{(c) The p-value}
For the observed positive contrast, the least favorable null p-value is
\[
\boxed{p=1-\Phi(1/\sqrt2)
=\tfrac12\Pr(\chi_1^2\geq0.5)\approx0.239750.}
\]
The mixture calibration is exact here because the two sample means
are normal and the common variance is known.
